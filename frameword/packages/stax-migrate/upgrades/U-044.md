# U-044: measure the foot the way it actually fails

TARGET: {{TARGET}}
REFERENCE: `e2e/foot-law.spec.ts` in the reference app

## DETECT

The foot law is "44px, one line, at every width". The three obvious assertions
are all blind to the way it really breaks. Run them and watch them pass:

- `.panel-foot` height: 44.00, because a wrapped label hides inside a 30px
  button whenever `line-height: 1` lets two 12px lines fit.
- `.panel-foot` scrollWidth minus clientWidth: 0, because the overrun lives in a
  DESCENDANT and never reaches the foot's own scroll box.
- children sharing one centre line: true, because the wrap is inside ONE child.

All three pass while the foot paints three baselines and buries a control.

Then check the ladder itself: grep each responsive rung's selector against what
the app actually renders. A rung aimed at a class nobody emits has never fired.

## APPLY

1. Foot buttons carry `white-space: nowrap` and do not shrink. A squeezed flex
   child otherwise collapses to its longest word and the label breaks.
2. ONE element absorbs the pressure, and it is the one that can: the view deck
   scrolls. Check its base rule for `flex: none`, which silently defeats any
   `min-width: 0` added for this purpose.
3. A deck that yields by scrolling must not squeeze its own tabs on the way.
4. Every rung must target a class the app emits. Wrap the label if the rung
   sheds a label. Delete rungs whose selector matches nothing.

## VERIFY

Three assertions, and only these three see it:

- LINE BOXES BY VERTICAL POSITION. `Range.getClientRects()` per text node,
  counting distinct rounded tops. Counting the RECTS is wrong: a text node
  starting with a space gets its own rect for that space on the same line, so
  "6 rows" reports two rects and no wrap.
- DESCENDANT overflow, walked. Exempt anything that ellipsises or scrolls:
  yielding under pressure is the ladder working.
- A HIT TEST on every foot control via `elementFromPoint` at its own centre. A
  control painted under a neighbour is not a control.

Run all three across every surface, at eight widths, in both themes.
